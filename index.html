Research

Factor Investing in Corporate Bond Mutual Funds
Job Market Paper

Abstract: “Despite the widely recognized importance of factors in asset pricing and the rapid growth of factor investing, little is known about how factor strategies are implemented in corporate bond portfolios. Using detailed holdings of U.S. corporate bond mutual funds, I provide novel evidence on their factor exposures and associated performance implications. I find that funds generally tilt against well-known academic factors, and these contrarian tilts are highly persistent. Although managers capture significant premia through several factor tilts at the bond-holdings level, these premia often fail to translate into superior fund performance. Instead, funds that tilt toward the factors generally underperform peers that tilt against them. Liquidity management costs and heterogeneous portfolio allocations, such as cash, government bonds, and Rule 144A bonds, significantly offset the return benefits of factor investing. Overall, these findings highlight the importance of implementation frictions in shaping both managerial behavior and the practical value of factor investing in the corporate bond market.”

Mutual Fund Tournament and ESG Ratings
with Fabio Moneta
SSRN

Abstract: “We document a novel form of tournament behavior based on ESG: U.S. equity growth fund managers use Morningstar’s ESG Risk Ratings (measured by a five-globe system) as a competitive tool to attract investor flows. We provide evidence consistent with a causal link between past performance and ESG tilts: funds that underperform early in the year strategically rebalance toward higher-ESG-rated stocks and sell non-ESG stocks to improve their sustainability ratings by year-end. These portfolio tilts do not enhance financial returns or environmental outcomes, yet investors reward them: underperforming funds that succeed in raising their globe ratings experience smaller outflows than those that do not. A similar pattern emerges among European growth funds, though the tournament instead centers on Morningstar’s Low Carbon Designation. Our findings raise ethical concerns that such ESG tilts may reflect agency-driven incentives rather than a genuine commitment to investors’ interests or sustainable impact.”

Retail Investor and Stock Return Predictability
with Fabio Moneta

Abstract: “We examine the return predictability of retail order flow over the 2014-2022 period. Retail order flow predicts stock returns for several weeks during 2014-2016, but this relation declines substantially during 2017-2022. The early-period predictability is concentrated among small, low-liquidity stocks and episodes of strong retail buying pressure. Cross-sectional, within-stock, and time-series analyses consistently show that retail return predictability is stronger when liquidity is lower. Improved liquidity facilitates more rapid absorption of retail-driven demand shocks, weakening their persistence and associated return predictability. These findings are more consistent with a friction-based explanation than with superior retail information.”
